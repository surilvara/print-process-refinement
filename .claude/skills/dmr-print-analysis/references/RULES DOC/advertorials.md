# GENERAL GUIDELINES
For an ADVERTORIAL, we divide the page into the product categories featured (e.g. watches, jewelry, personal objects).
Note: different jewelry plines should not be divided

If the article has a main focus (when taking into account the title, the caption, the prominence of the items), the full page is assigned to the main focus.

Cut shots and Showcases (with a model wearing the items) are not treated differently.

ADVERTORIALS 　– advertorial / TIE UP
Advertorials are advertising spaces purchased by advertisers and characterized by some specific features.
DMR advertorial classification includes;
	JP Advertorial : 	pages created by advertisers (Exact same articles in different magazines)
	JP Tie-up:		pages created by the magazine
They are usually completely attributed to the brand paying for them while any mention of other brands is omitted.

OBVIOUS FEATURES :
 «promotion»
 «PR»
 «advertising section»
 «advertorial» «広告» «特別広告企画»
 «advertisment»
 «publicité»
 «sponsord by» «提供» «協力»
 «presented by»
 Logo at the corner
 QR code
 «Title x Brand»

LESS OBVIOUS FEATURES:
 Different layout from any other regular editorial content
 regular columns or features, but same brands are covered
 The presence of CONTACT INFORMATION «お問い合わせ先»   (such as phone number or website) placed at the end of the article or just below the title identifies a Tie-up any time the magazine has a specific address page.
Present or event invitation sponsored by the brand. (the address or website for applying  them are brand’s official address or website)

EXCEPTIONS:
All regular columns normally focused on different brands
All articles with contact info if the magazine has no specific address page
-    Present or event invitation sponsored by magazine. (the address or website for applying them are publisher’s office or website.)
-    In trade association advertorial pages (eg. Woolmark, Verocuoio, World Gold Coucil, DTC, Cotton USA, etc), all the other brands pictured and captioned have to be registered as editorials and evaluated for SPACE OCCUPIED

## JAPAN ADVERTORIAL FLOWCHART — Updated Version (2017/2018)

*Source: Japan_ADVERTORIAL.pdf — supersedes earlier Japan flowchart for One Brand Articles*

The flowchart below applies to **JAPAN ONLY** for single-brand articles. It replaces the 2016 version with the same structure:

**Starting point: One Brand Article**

1. **With Obvious Feature?**
   - YES → **Advertorial**
   - NO → continue

2. **Different Layout vs. other regular editorial articles?**
   - YES → go to step 3a
   - NO → go to step 3b

3a. (Different layout + no obvious feature) **Is there any contact info?** (phone / brand official web address)
   - YES → go to step 4a
   - NO → **Editorial**

3b. (Same layout + no obvious feature) **Is there any contact info?**
   - YES → go to step 4b
   - NO → **Editorial**

4a / 4b. **Is it a regular column and different brands are featured?**
   - YES → **Editorial**
   - NO → **Advertorial**

*Note: The 2017 flowchart is functionally identical to the 2016 version for Japan. The key clarification added is that "contact info" means phone number OR brand's official web address — not the publisher's address.*

**Additional less-obvious feature added in 2017/2018:**
- Present or event invitation **sponsored by the brand** (the address/website for applying is the brand's official address/website) → counts as tie-up/advertorial
- Present or event invitation sponsored by the **publisher/magazine** (address is publisher's) → Editorial

## JAPAN: PEN MAGAZINE — New Rule (2020)

*Source: Japan_new_advertorial_rule_for_PEN_magazine.pptx — applies to PEN magazine only*

PEN was previously treated as a magazine without an indirizzi page (i.e., contact info = NOT a tie-up indicator). **This rule was updated in 2020** with a new exception within PEN:

### New PEN flowchart (2020):

Starting from the standard Japan flowchart, when the article has contact info and is NOT a regular column, add one more check before classifying as Advertorial:

**Is the article mentioned on the contents page AND is there a page number credited on the pages?**
- **YES** → **Editorial** (the article is indexed in the sommario and carries a page number — it is treated as legitimate editorial content despite the contact info)
- **NO** → **Advertorial**

### Worked examples from PEN April 2019:

| Article | Contact info? | In contents? | Page number on page? | Classification |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Louis Vuitton article | Yes | Yes (mentioned in contents) | Yes (page number credited) | **Editorial** |
| Issey Miyake article | Yes | No (not mentioned in contents) | No (no page number) | **Advertorial** |

### Implication:
For PEN specifically, a one-brand article with contact info is **only an advertorial** if it is **not indexed in the sommario** and **carries no page number on its pages**. If both conditions are met (in contents + page number), classify as editorial despite the contact info.

This changes the previous blanket rule that PEN = no indirizzi page = contact info irrelevant. The 2020 rule instead uses sommario presence + page numbering as the tie-break.
