# General Rules — DMR Print

**Source:** MANUALE REGOLE GENERALI (official DMR training manual)

Topics covered: publications with embedded translations, "Le Aziende Informano" sections, publiredazionali (advertorials), tie-ups in Japanese magazines, sponsored magazines.

---

## 1. Publications with Embedded Translations

Some magazines include partial or complete translations of articles in one or more languages. Three scenarios apply:

### Scenario 1 — Translation at the back of the magazine, text only
**→ NOT counted.**
The translation section is a faithful text-only reproduction of content already in the magazine. Example: Numéro France always has an English translation section at the back with a separate summary — this is entirely ignored.

### Scenario 2 — Translation on the facing page (opposite the original)
- If **text only** → **NOT counted.**
- If it adds **new images** compared to the original → **valued as FULL PAGE.**

Example (Style & The Family Tunes Germany, p.42/43): p.43 is a faithful text translation of p.42. No new images. Not counted.

Example (Style & The Family Tunes Germany, p.18/19): p.19 is a translation of p.18 but **adds new images**. It is a Reebok monography → entered as **2 FP – MONO**.
- Note: for non-monographic articles, each brand in the new images is valued for the space it occupies.

### Scenario 3 — Translation on the same page as the original text
**→ NOT counted.** The original-language text is treated as if it occupies the full page; the translation portion is ignored.

Example (Style & The Family Tunes Germany, p.34): page has German (original) + English (translation). The German part spreads across the full page. It is a G-Star monography → entered as **1 FP – MONO**.
- Note: for non-monographic articles, each brand is valued for the space it occupies.

## 2. "Le Aziende Informano" Sections

These are editorial sections produced by the magazine's editorial staff (not paid advertising). Brands/products are valued for the **space they occupy**.

### Space calculation:
- Divide the page equally among all boxes/items.
- Examples:
  - 9 equal-size boxes → 1/9 per brand
  - 5 equal-size boxes → 1/5 per brand
  - 3 boxes on 2/3 of the page → 1/5 per brand (3 boxes on 2/3 ÷ total page)

## 3. Publiredazionali (Advertorials)

An advertorial is a paid advertising space characterized by specific identifiers. The entire space is attributed to the paying brand; any other brand citations are omitted.

**Exception:** In advertorials from **trade associations** (e.g., Woolmark, Verocuoio, World Gold Council, DTC, Cotton USA), any brands present must be entered as **editorial** and valued for space occupied.

### Primary identifiers (any one is sufficient to classify as publiredazionale):
- Text label: "messaggio promozionale", "informazione pubblicitaria", "advertising section", "advertorial", "publicité", "promotion", "sponsored by", "presented by"
- Brand logo prominently displayed
- "[Magazine name] × [Brand]" format (except Japanese magazines — see §4)
- Barcode present
- Chinese equivalents: 廣編特輯, 特約專輯, 全頁廣告, 商稿, 專刊, 廣編特企
- Other identifiers: "Agenda — advertising, promotion, events, opportunities"; "Tod's Special"; "Information provided by [Brand]"; "[Magazine] Release"

### Secondary identifiers (used when no primary identifier is present):
- Layout clearly different from normal editorial pages
- Brand contact info directly on the page (when the magazine already has a dedicated indirizzi/addresses page)

### Decision flowchart:
1. Is there a primary identifier? → **YES** → Publiredazionale. **NO** → continue.
2. Is the layout different from normal editorial pages? → **YES** → Publiredazionale. **NO** → continue.
3. Are there editorial details (Redattore/Fotografo)? → **NO** → Publiredazionale. **YES** → continue.
4. Is the editor's name among the usual contributors? → **YES** → Redazionale. **NO** → Publiredazionale.

### Entering publiredazionali:
- Full space attributed to the paying brand with PR flag.
- Other brand citations in the same space: **omitted** (with the trade association exception above).
- Example: Woolmark "Communiqué Vogue" (8 pages) → 8 FP with PR flag to Woolmark; other brands present on those pages entered as editorial for space occupied.

## 4. Tie-Ups in Japanese Magazines

Tie-ups are pages entirely dedicated to a single brand. They have an editorial (monographic) structure but must be classified as **publiredazionali**.

### How to identify a tie-up:
The presence of **contact info** (phone number, website) at the end of the service or directly under the title is the identifying feature — especially when the magazine already has a dedicated addresses page.

### Exceptions (NOT tie-ups, treat as editorial monographies):
1. **Recurring brand-rotation sections** — e.g., "BAILA'S TIPS" (Baila Japan): a fixed section that features a different brand each issue. Despite having contact info, it is editorial because it is a recurring format.
2. **Magazines without a dedicated addresses page** — e.g., PEN Japan, Brutus, Casa Brutus. Even if contact info appears at the end of an article, these are treated as editorial monographies because contact info there serves a structural purpose (the magazine has no indirizzi page).
3. **Watch magazines that list contact info at the end of every article** — e.g., WWW Japan, Watch Navi, Udedokei-ou, Tokei Begin, Chronos. If the service has the same layout as all other pages, it is NOT a publiredazionale.

## 5. Sponsored Magazines

Sponsored magazines are supplements or special issues characterized by a **sponsor brand present on every page**, both editorial and advertising.

### Rule: All pages are attributed to the sponsor brand as FULL PAGE PUBLIREDAZIONALI.

### Variant — sponsor brand + other-sector mentions:
If the magazine also contains editorial mentions of brands from **different sectors** than the sponsor:
- Sponsor brand: all pages → full page publiredazionale
- Other-sector brands: entered for space occupied as **editorial**
- Same-sector brand citations: **omitted**

Example (L'Oreal Paris & Elle supplement): all pages → L'Oreal Paris publiredazionale; fashion brand mentions in editorial pages → entered as editorial for space occupied.

### Important distinctions — NOT the same as sponsored magazines:

**"In association with" supplements:**
These are normal editorial supplements simply co-published with a brand. The brand name appears on the cover (e.g., "in association with H&M"). They have normal editorial structure with multiple brands in both advertising and editorial pages.
- Treat as a normal supplement.
- Attribute each brand's ADV, PR, and editorial space normally.
- Wherever the "associate" brand's logo is visible → assign **1/20 editorial**.

**Advertising catalogue booklets:**
Booklets that have no magazine reference on the cover. These are not sponsored supplements — they are **advertising inserts** (inserti ADV). Enter as such.

## 6. Japan/Korea — "Sponsored" Label Ubiquity Issue [Updated: 2026-04]

*Source: session-5 transcript (Antonella Genovese + Mariana Martella)*

In Japanese and Korean magazines, the labels "Sponsored" and "In Collaboration With" appear very widely — including on articles that in other markets would be clearly editorial. This creates a classification challenge.

**Antonella's rule (operationally applied):**

The key distinction is not the label alone, but the **editorial structure** of the page:

- If the page with "Sponsored" or "In Collaboration With" has:
  - A named redattore and fotografo who appear in the magazine's staff
  - A page number
  - Appears in the sommario
  - Layout consistent with other editorial pages of the magazine
  
  → **Editorial**, despite the label.

- If the page with "Sponsored" has:
  - No redattore/fotografo (or they are not in the staff)
  - No page number
  - Not indexed in the sommario
  - Layout different from other editorial pages
  
  → **PR (Publiredazionale/Advertorial)**

**Operational reality:** Italy/international QC asks the Japan/Korea responsible operators (Noko, Giovanna, or China equivalent) to confirm whether a page is PR or not when ambiguous. Classifying based on publisher knowledge is common — e.g., Condé Nast Japan (Vogue Japan) is known to always use "Sponsored" even on editorial content and is NOT treated as PR unless the structure is clearly different.

**Pending revision:** Antonella has flagged this area as needing an updated unified rule. The current approach is a case-by-case check. France has also indicated intent to revise. Any change can only be implemented at semester or year boundary (not mid-period).

## 7. PR (Publiredazionale) — When Label is Absent [Updated: 2026-04]

*Source: session-5 transcript (Antonella Genovese)*

A specific operational rule applies when an ADV page has editorial-style text (e.g., a Caudalie page describing a face treatment, with the product) but **no PR label**:

**Rule: If there is no label identifying the content as paid ("advertorial", "informazione pubblicitaria", etc.), the page is entered as ADV (advertising), not as PR.**

Rationale: The reader's point of view applies. If a reader cannot identify the page as paid content (no label), it is treated as advertising by the ADV team — the PR classification requires the label.

**Practical consequence:** The same brand (e.g., Caudalie) may accumulate pages as both PR (when labelled) and pure ADV (when not labelled) across different issues. This is intentional and expected. The distinction matters less for MIV calculations than for ensuring no pages are missed.

**Adjacent case:** When a Caudalie ADV (no label) appears side-by-side with a Caudalie PR (labelled), on facing pages:
- If no insert/cartonata is involved: ADV team enters both as ADV if no label. If the PR label is present on one: PR for that one, ADV for the other.
- The editorial team does NOT pick these up (pure advertising context).
