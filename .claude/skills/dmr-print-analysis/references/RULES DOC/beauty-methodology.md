MONOGRAPHY
We define monographies as articles, columns and features entirely dedicated to one or more brands of the same Company that may cover one or more pages, except for newspapers that can occupy even less than an entire page.

PRODUCT MONOGRAPHIC ARTICLES
The monographic article is focused on one product and one or more product lines, often accompanied by text. The value to be assigned will be the total space that also includes the text part. Regardless if it’s a worn article or a still life, the whole space can be attributed to the product/product line and any references to other products of the same brand are omitted.

CORPORATE MONOGRAPHIC ARTICLES
Articles about the institutional sphere of the company/brand: events, sponsorships, interviews, etc.
Usually, the space is entirely attributed to the corporate category unless there is a specific product, photographed and described. In this case, the space occupied by the product is  considered separated from the space relative to the institutional sphere.
If the article is about the launch of a product, even if it is structured as event or interview, the space is entirely attributed to the product launched.

TESTIMONIAL MONOGRAPHIC ARTICLES
In an article about a testimonial with a space dedicated to the product that he/she is representing, we evaluate it separately from the text.

TESTIMONIAL MONOGRAPHY (REQUIREMENTS)

An article on celebrity/testimonials is considered a Monography and therefore is entirely attributed to a brand, if and only if it fulfills AT LEAST ONE of the following REQUIREMENTS
 there has to be the quote of the brand in relation to the celebrity in the title and/or subtitle; moreover this relationship must be mentioned at least once in the text or the "contact info" at the end of the article.
(Note: if the celebrity is linked to more than one brand in the title and/or subtitle, each of the brands will be given only the space occupied unless the text is then entirely dedicated to one of these)

 there have to be the pictures of the product that the celebrity represents and/or it has to be clearly specified that the pictures of the story were provided by the brand.

 there have to be the pictures of the advertising campaign that the subject did for the brand and the relationship between the brand and the celebrity has to be clearly specified.

 there has to be an unquestionable reference to the fact that the story was organized in cooperation with the press office of the brand to whom the celebrity is connected.
(ex. The testimonial’s interview has been realized in the showroom of the brand or the PR or corporate of the brand are in  attendance during the interview with the celebrity)

INTERVIEW to a member of the company (owner/make-up artist) with regards to his/her role and/or business of the company. It has to be specified that it’s a CORPORATE INTERVIEW associating the specific product of the character or, if it doesn’t exist, the generic one (beauty interview).
CORPORATE - PEOPLE
 gossip on company’s members
 interview to make-up artist, owner or other employee of the company, with regards to his/her private life
 picture of someone related to the company at a party (party/runway and/or another brand event)
 relatives of a character related to a brand
When a section of the text is related to corporate and another to the corporate character, everything is inserted under the section that occupies more space.
CORPORATE SOCIAL RESPONSIBILITY
 all initiatives focused on inclusivity, sustainability, gender equality, environment
 text and/or pictures of products realized with a social or fundraising purpose
ECONOMIC NEWS
 economic trend of the company
 mergers, acquisitions and joint venture
 stock news (if the company is listed)

PROD LINES
STORES
 text/images on a brand’s store or a brand’s corner in a department store

UP-TO-DATE NEWS

 ERRATA CORRIGE
EVENTS
 text or pictures of parties, commemoration, acknowledgement or other pr events
SPONSORSHIP
 initiative sponsored by a brand for a cultural and/or sporting effort, from which the brand itself benefits from an image standpoint
DIFFERENCE BETWEEN EVENTS AND SPONSORSHIP:
SPONSORSHIP is a “Do ut des” meaning that the brand supports an initiative (cultural, social and/or sporting) not necessarily connected to its core business and receves from it a benefit from an image standpoint.
An EVENT is developed by a brand for reasons appropriate to its core business.
TESTIMONIAL
 a celebrity chosen from a brand as a testimonial of its image
CAUTION: if the celebrity is described inside an adv campaign and significant attention is given to him/her, we use the Prod Line “advertising campaign”
NOTE: for testimonials, the name has to be associated in the product field (if existing) and in the “celebrities” field (true even if the data is inserted under “advertising campaign”)
